# Dogtra 1900X — nomenclature checked against the manual

**Source of record:** *1900X 1-DOG UNIT Owner's Manual*, Dogtra, 48pp
(`USA_1900X_6.pdf`, downloaded from dogtra.com/pages/manuals 2026-08-06).
Local copies: `_work/1900X-manual.pdf`, text at `_work/manual-1900X.txt`.

**Unit of record:** the owner's own collar — rear label reads
`1900X · 1-DOG UNIT · 126072`, `ISED 12166A-TD33UT`, `FCC SWN-TD33UT`, Made in
Korea. Photographs `_work/photos/IMG_4200–4213`.

The **1900S** manual is a *different generation* and must not be used. It has a
0–127 dial and a Nick/Constant toggle switch; the 1900X has a 0–100 dial and a
programmable front button. Several search results lead to the 1900S — ignore them.

## 3. Numbers the app must get right

- **100 stimulation levels** (p.7) — *not* 127. The 127 figure belongs to the 1900S.
- Range 3/4 mile; Black Edition 1 mile (p.7, p.29).
- Constant cuts out automatically at **12 seconds** as a safety feature (p.3).
- Boosted Constant, XPP Vibration, Tone, Locate-hold: each up to 12 s (p.14, p.17).
- Indicator light flashes **every 4 seconds** when on and ready (p.33).
- Battery colours: green full · amber medium · red needs charge (p.33).
- Safety lock: press the switch on top of the Rheostat Dial for **more than
  2 seconds**; a lock icon appears on the LCD (p.18).
- Boost maths: base displayed level + incremental amount = output (p.14).
- LCD may go dim or lag below **10 °F**; the unit still works (p.34).
- 2-hour rapid charge (p.7).

## 4. Fitting — what the manual actually says (p.27)

Three targets, and only three:

1. Contact points **press firmly against the dog's skin**.
2. You should be able to fit **two fingers** underneath the collar strap.
3. Best location is **on either side of the dog's windpipe**.

Plus the wear limits, which are a welfare rule and belong in the app:
reposition the receiver **every few hours**, and **remove it after 8 hours of
use**. Check for skin irritation every time.

Failure mode the manual names: a loose fit lets the receiver move, the contacts
rub and irritate, and contact becomes inconsistent — so the *corrections* become
inconsistent.

## 4b. Coat depth — the manual's remedy is NOT longer posts (p.43)

This one has already been got wrong once, so read it carefully.

The troubleshooting entry "My dog is not reacting to the system" gives four
checks, in order: both units on; the strap tight enough that both contacts touch
skin; the contacts may be **too short for a thick or long coat**, in which case
the manual's remedy is to **trim the hair on the dog's neck** so both contacts
reach skin; and only then, that the level may be too low.

**The manual nowhere says longer contact points are supplied.** An earlier draft
of tab 3 taught "longer posts are supplied for a thick coat; use them rather than
pulling the strap tighter". That invents a product remedy Dogtra does not offer
here *and* omits the one it does. Do not teach it.

Note the order, because it is the whole lesson of tab 3: Dogtra puts *contact with
skin* above *raising the level*. Turning the dial up on a dog that physically
cannot feel the posts is the failure this tab exists to prevent, and the manual
itself ranks the checks that way.

## 4c. What a response looks like — manual-sourced (p.43)

Tab 4's tells were pure doctrine. The manual actually supplies some: keep
increasing until the dog responds, "usually by neck movement, head shaking,
looking over his shoulder", and it warns the response **may be very subtle**.

So neck movement, head shake and looking over the shoulder can be cited to the
manual. Ear flick, glance away, never-a-yelp and never-a-flinch remain Highland
Canine doctrine and must still be marked as such. Do not blur the two.

## 5. Finding the level — what the manual actually says (p.31)

The manual's whole procedure is three sentences: always start at the lowest
level and work up; you have found the right level when the dog responds with a
**mild response**; expect to adjust as the dog's arousal and distraction change.

That is all the manual gives. Everything finer-grained in tab 4 — ear flick,
head turn, glance away, never a yelp, never a flinch — is *training doctrine*,
not manual text, and the app must not present it as manufacturer instruction.

## Discrepancies — flag these, do not silently pick a side

**D1 · "Dog selector" vs "Receiver Selector Toggle."**
Course slides and the previous build say *dog selector toggle*. The manual
(p.8) says **Receiver Selector Toggle (Up to 3-Dogs)**. Both describe the same
switch. Ship the manual's name as the heading, keep the course phrase as an
alias so a student searching for it still finds it.

**D2 · What the contact points are made of.**
p.27 contradicts itself inside one page: the *Improper Fit* paragraph credits
"medical grade stainless steel contact points and anti-microbial plastic", the
*Proper Fit* paragraph asks that "the anti-microbial plastic contact points"
press against the skin. The posts on the owner's unit are plainly metal. Say
metal posts in a plastic housing and note that the manual is inconsistent.

**D3 · The antenna.**
p.6 and p.21 mark both Antenna and Antenna Hinge with the asterisk "Included
only with 1900X BLACK EDITION". The owner's unit is **not** a Black Edition —
the Black Edition is matte black body *and* strap (p.10–11), and this unit is
two-tone tan/black — yet it plainly carries a screw-in antenna
(IMG_4210, IMG_4213). So either the asterisk covers only the *detachable*
antenna accessory, or the standard unit ships with one anyway. State the
observed fact; do not repeat the asterisk as if it settled the matter.
The manual also misspells it **"Antena"** twice on p.6.

**D4 · Locate Light hold behaviour — the previous build is wrong.**
`E-COLLAR-3D/parts.js` says press-and-hold gives "a steady light lasting up to
12 seconds". The manual (p.17) says press and hold makes it **stay blinking**
for up to 12 seconds. Tap once = continuous blink every 4 s; tap again = off.
Fix this.

**D5 · Stimulation range.**
Any course slide showing 0–127 is the 1900S. This unit is **0–100**.

**D6 · "Working level" is not manual vocabulary.**
The manual says *"the right stimulation level"* and *"a mild response"*
(p.31). Highland Canine teaches *working level* / *recognition level*. Keep the
course vocabulary in the UI — it is what the student will be examined on — but
say once, in the tab, that the manual's own phrase is "the right stimulation
level" so the student is not caught out by the manual's wording.

**D7 · The transmitter on the older course slide is a different generation.**
Already noted in the previous build and still true. The slide's diagram has a
Nick/Constant *toggle*; the 1900X has one programmable front button (p.13).
