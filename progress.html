    Critic: Not shippable. Back to the builder.

    Biggest remaining gap: Cloud opacity is binary — edges cut from opaque to transparent in 1-3 px with stair-stepping, and no cloud self-shadows. No silver lining anywhere near the sun. Reads as painted decals on a gradient dome.

  • Grass

    Back to builder

    bar: three.js instanced-grass examples round 2

    Critic: Not shippable. Back to the builder.

    Biggest remaining gap: Density inverts with distance: the mat opens into separated tufts on bare soil through the middle band, then closes again at the horizon. Real grass hides the ground MORE as it recedes. Also a monoculture — one species, one height, one hue.

  • The trainer

    Back to builder

    look and movement — must read as a real man round 2

    Critic: Not shippable. Back to the builder.

    Biggest remaining gap: Arms swing on the SAME side as the legs. Human gait is strictly contralateral, and this is the error a lay viewer feels instantly as 'marching mannequin'. Also the trailing foot dangles fully pointed on a straight knee, which happens at no point in a real gait cycle.

Log

  • Bar set: three.js's own sky and grass examples are the pass mark; Red Dead Redemption 2 is the direction, not the bar.
  • Gates written down before any building: 60fps, zero network, single-file build still works.
  • Round 1 builders sent out on all four pieces in parallel, each into its own prototype so they cannot collide.
  • Captured the bar: three.js sky example at 1560x852, both at its sunset default and re-shot at a 38-degree sun so the comparison is like-for-like with a daytime field.
  • Captured our own scene at three framings to be the 'before' half of each blind pair.
  • Searched all 588 three.js examples: there is no grass demo. webgl_instancing_scatter is the closest, so the grass bar is softer than the sky bar.
  • Sky: THREE.Sky is absent from r128, so it was vendored. Preetham scattering, cloud deck, crepuscular rays, aerial perspective sharing the sky's own scatter function. 2.19 ms GPU at 1600x1000 on an M5.
  • Grass: 206,000 blades, 902k triangles, 5 draw calls, 6.5 ms. Blades carry a cell index rather than a position, so they stay pinned to the world while the buffer stays pinned to the camera.
  • Trainer: no redistributable rigged humanoid exists that is worth using - three.js's good ones are Mixamo-licensed, the free ones are a cartoon robot and a low-poly mannequin. So the scan was kept and given a real 26-bone rig, the fused trousers cut along the surface equidistant from both leg chains, and a walk driven from the planted foot with the pelvis height derived. Foot slip 0.00 mm.
  • CORRECTION, and it is mine: the dog builder measured 90.2% paw slip in the CURRENT shipped gait. My earlier ground-lock test verified the phase clock against distance, not the foot against the ground - the stance sweep runs the wrong way and its amplitude is too small. The rebuild measures 4.2%.
  • VOID: the daytime sky bar was blown out to near-white (RGB 224-255, 31 levels of range). I raised the sun and exposure in the example's own GUI and shipped the result without looking at it.
  • VOID: the grass bar was not grass. three.js has no grass demo; instancing-scatter scatters coloured shapes over a blob surface. I picked it off a filename search.
  • New gate added, because this is the failure the whole method warns about: every bar must be an image somebody has actually opened and looked at before a critic is allowed to judge against it.
  • Grass has no bar C at all — three.js ships nothing comparable — so grass is promoted to bar A, Red Dead Redemption 2, which is where it should have been from the start.
  • Sky bar reverts to the example's own shipped default, which captured correctly (RGB 48-255). Ours gets matched to that sun rather than dragging the bar to ours.
  • Critics found real faults in our work regardless: same-side arm swing on the man, a pace fragment on the dog, binary cloud edges on the sky, inverted density on the grass. 0 of 4 shippable.
